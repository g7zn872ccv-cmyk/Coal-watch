How to read this data

HCC, WHC, CRN — benchmark & producers

Closest thing to a direct coal-price read here. These are pure-play met coal miners, so their stock tends to move with the price they realize. Watch for all three moving together — that's a real signal. A single name moving alone is more likely company-specific noise (earnings, an outage, M&A chatter) or just general stock-market swings than a coal-price move.

FXI, INDA, EWJ — country ETFs

Broad equity-market proxies for China, India, and Japan — financials, tech, consumer stocks and all. A rising ETF means that country's stock market is doing well in general, not that steel or coal demand specifically is rising. Treat these as macro/sentiment context, not a demand signal.

BHP

A diversified miner with real exposure to iron ore and copper, met coal a smaller slice these days. Reads as "global commodity demand is healthy or not" more than anything coal-specific.

PKX (POSCO)

An actual steelmaker, so this one's a legitimate demand read for Korean steel output — unlike the ETFs above.

CAD/USD

Met coal is sold in USD. A weaker Canadian dollar is a margin tailwind for a BC exporter even if the coal price itself hasn't moved — rising USD/CAD (CAD weakening) helps; falling hurts.

China, India, Japan Manufacturing PMI

Above 50 means the manufacturing sector is expanding month-over-month; below 50 means contracting. These feed steel demand with a lag — a strengthening PMI today suggests firmer steel (and by extension coking coal) demand in the following months, not immediately. Note: the China PMI tracked here was branded "Caixin" for years and was renamed "RatingDog" in 2026 — same S&P Global survey, just a new sponsor name, so don't mistake it for a different indicator if you see that name elsewhere.

Coking coal port inventory

Rising stockpiles generally mean demand is soft relative to supply (coal is piling up unsold); falling stockpiles mean demand is outpacing what's arriving — a tightening signal that often precedes price strength.

General caveat

The actual PLV coal benchmark and raw iron ore swaps aren't available through free embeddable widgets — everything above is the closest free substitute, not the real thing. Useful for direction and context, not for precise price levels.

Widgets are provided by TradingView and reflect live market data, not investment advice. The underlying PLV/iron-ore swap contracts aren't available on the free embed tier — producer and ETF tickers throughout are the working substitutes for those.
